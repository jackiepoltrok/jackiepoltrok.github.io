# Research Question
### How do different mobile photogrammetry applications digitally represent the same material object, and what do their differences reveal about the possibilities and limitations of 3D modelling in digital archaeology?
# Connection to Archaeological Theory
# Data Collection
#### Camera Details
Sony Cyber-shot DSC-P32 / 3.2MP / Digital Camera. Made and released in 2003, making it 23 years old.

---

The camera first belonged to my mother. I found it five years ago while cleaning out bins in our basement. It shockingly turned on, and I was able to find photos of my sister and I that it had saved from 2007. It was the camera that my mother used to document me and my sister's childhood.
I chose to use this camera to create a digital model for a variety of reasons. First, it is a very intricate object, with many small buttons and divots and details that could be challenging for a 3D model app to pick up. Second, a camera is in itself a digital archaology tool. Cameras are used to collect data in the format of photos that are used to digitally keep record of memories and artifacts, and of course, to create digital 3D models. And lastly, this camera is historically significant to me. It was passed down to me from my mother who used it in the early 2000s to document me and my sister’s upbringing. As an object, it has its own history and is an archaeological item. But as a tool, it has been used to keep track of my history for years.

## The Collection Process
I selected one physical digital camera as the object of the study. If I were to have selected something like a sculpture on campus, I would risk not being able to take photos of every angle, and not having control of the lighting could have proven an issue as well. I know that natural lighting works better for using photogrammetry. I placed the camera on my kitchen table and followed the prompts of each application.

### KIRI Engine
Scan Mode: Photo Scan<br>
Polygon Counts: High<br>
Texture Resolution: 4k<br>
Date captured: October 20, 2026<br>
Location: Ottawa<br>
Video tool. I moved around the object with my phone camera while recording. No obvious captures were taken, not like photos.<br>

### Luma 3D
Date captured: October 20, 2026<br>
Location: Ottawa<br>
Prompted me to put the object inside a virtual box that appeared on my screen while pointing my camera at the object. Three rings were positioned around the object. One towards the top, one around the middle, and one at the bottom. I was to move around the object three times to fill each ring. There were placers where my phone should be displayed on the screen to ensure I hit every angle. 

### Polycam
Capture Method: free object mode<br>
Detail: medium<br>
Date captured: October 20, 2026<br>
Number of frames: 45<br>
Location: Ottawa<br>
Notes: A video-like format. Took captures as my phone camera moved around the object until it prompted me that had enough to make a model.<br>

# Comparison Criteria

| Criteria | What I am examining |
|---|---|
| Shape and structre | Does the model accurately reproduce the camera's overall form? |
| Surface detail | Are buttons, lenses, edges and other features visible? |
| Texture | Does the model capture the camera's surface and material appearance? |
| Missing info | Are there gaps, holes or areas that were not reconstructed? |
| Distortion | Are any parts stretched, warped or incorrectly shaped? |
| File quality | What file formats and levels of detail does each app produce? |
